Tito was sitting in a tub of lukewarm water on the bathroom floor.

The water came up just past the edge of her shell, warm enough to feel like a bath and not a cup of tea. Her neck was stretched out and her eyes were half closed. For days she'd barely moved, barely eaten, and hadn't pooped at all.

That's the bit nobody tells you about having a tortoise. You end up caring a lot about poop. Tito was constipated, properly constipated, and when something that small and that slow gets sick, it's hard to tell whether she's resting or getting worse.

So we went to the vet. Tito came home with medicine, a routine, and instructions for warm water soaks. I'd fill the tub, test it on my wrist like you would a baby's bottle, lower her in, then sit on the edge of the bathtub and wait.

For a few days nothing happened. Same tired tortoise, same still water, same me checking my phone and then checking her again. I kept telling myself I was being patient, but really I was starting to panic.

Then one morning she pooped. Right there in the tub. Ha! I've never been so happy to clean something up in my life.

She didn't ask if it was going to work out. She didn't wait for a sign. She just got on with it.

I think about those days a lot. All those mornings I thought nothing was happening, something was happening. The medicine, the warm water, her own little body quietly fixing itself, it just wasn't happening where I could see it.

And the energy that got Tito moving again is the same energy that turns seeds into forests. Tortoises have been plodding around this planet for over 200 million years, outlasting the dinosaurs without ever once being in a hurry. It's in you too. Right now every cell in your body is doing a city's worth of work every second, building and fixing and sending messages back and forth, and you didn't have to ask a single one of them to start.

And yet here I am at 2am staring at the ceiling. Those nights it was Tito, picturing her getting worse, picturing the empty tub under the sink. And when it's not Tito it's something else. The application gets rejected, the thing I built gets ignored, the conversation I've had tried went sideways.

I can play these films in my head in high definition. With sound. With the exact words people will use when they tell me no, and the exact look on their face when they say it.

That's a lot of imagination. Honestly, it's impressive how much detail I can pour into a disaster that hasn't happened yet. If I put that much effort into the actual work, I'd probably have it finished by now.

So feel the fear. Sure. It's real and it's yours and pretending it isn't there only makes it louder.

But realise most of it is imagination pointed the wrong way. Like a torch shining at your own feet instead of the path in front of you. You can see your shoes really well, but you can't see where you're going.

What if you pointed it somewhere else? At the next idea instead of the next failure. At a way out of the mess you're in, or a better way to sit with it while it passes.

The nights I stopped imagining Tito getting worse and started imagining how to make her comfortable were the nights I actually got up and refilled the tub. Same muscle. Better use. That's a fight worth having with yourself.

I still remember what the vet said that first day.

How long has she been like this?

About a week.

She'll be alright. Tortoises are tougher than they look. They just do everything slowly. Including getting better.

So what do I do?

Keep her warm. Give her the medicine. Then give her time.

I carried Tito out to the car in a cardboard box and kept repeating it in my head. Tougher than they look. Slowly, including getting better. Give her time.

Yes yes yes... I know. Things do go wrong. Some medicine doesn't work, some pets don't pull through, and some of those 2am films turn out to be accurate.

Think about everything that had to happen for you to be standing where you are. Rivers carving valleys for thousands of years. Stars burning out to make the iron in your blood. Grandparents and great-grandparents who packed whatever they could carry and went somewhere new with no idea how it would turn out.

Millions and millions of people and plants and tortoises and cells, all of them just getting on with it. None of them with a guarantee. And here we are, worried about an email.

I get lost in it too. I'm worried about the email too. But it helps to zoom out every now and then.

Tito's back to her old self now. She plods across the floor chasing the patch of sunlight as it moves through the afternoon, and the tub is back under the sink where it belongs. Every now and then she stops, stretches her neck out, and looks at me like none of it ever happened.

She'll never be fast. She'll never be in a hurry. But she didn't need to be fast to get better.

She just got on with it.
